\noindent We submit our revised research manuscript titled \textbf{``Atomic Ladder Execution: A Serializable Protocol for Multi-Agent Proxy Bidding in Real-Time Auction Platforms''} (Paper ID 1255) for consideration for publication in ICDDS 2026.

\vspace{0.5em}

\noindent This paper addresses the fundamental concurrency and consistency challenge of proxy bidding in real-time English auction platforms. While commercial auction platforms routinely utilize single-jump approximations that discard intermediate counter-bids, our work introduces the \textbf{Atomic Ladder Protocol}, which executes the entire multi-agent ladder resolution within a single relational database transaction. By enforcing a strict two-tier hierarchical row-locking discipline (the auction row followed by participant wallets ordered lexicographically by user identifier), the protocol guarantees serializability and deadlock freedom under arbitrary concurrent arrival orders. We provide formal mathematical proofs of Log Fidelity, Vickrey Equivalence (second-price convergence), Serializability, and a closed-form iteration bound.

\vspace{0.5em}

\noindent Furthermore, our empirical evaluation compares direct transactional row locking against a decoupled event-sourced Redis Stream sequencer on PostgreSQL~15 and Redis~7 across consistent completion criteria. At 100 concurrent virtual users, the sequenced pipeline sustains 975.0~bids/s ingress throughput at an 8.1~ms 95th-percentile latency, and 182.4~bids/s durable persistence throughput (a $6.1\times$ improvement in durable database commit throughput over the direct row-locking baseline), while preserving bit-exact log determinism and auditable relational fidelity. In response to reviewer feedback, this revision also incorporates an in-depth distributed systems analysis examining multi-node transactional consensus overhead, two-phase commit latency invariants, and horizontal partition routing.
